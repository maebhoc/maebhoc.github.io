# Engineering Effectiveness — Engagement Guide

How the 2-week engagement actually runs. Use this in proposals, scoping conversations, and as a reference during the engagement itself.

---

## Before Day 1

Agree scope with the sponsor before the engagement starts:
- How many teams or areas are in scope?
- Who are the key people to speak to? (EM names, tech lead names)
- What access is needed? (GitHub, Jira, CI/CD tooling, any existing dashboards)
- What format does the sponsor want for the final report? (Readout only, written report, both)
- Who is this report for — the sponsor privately, or a wider leadership group?

Send a pre-assessment async form to engineering managers at this stage. Gets them thinking before Day 1 and gives early signal before the first interviews.

---

## Week 1 — Discovery, data mapping, conversations

### Day 1

- Kick-off call with the sponsor (30–60 mins)
- Confirm scope, access, and who is available when
- Begin data landscape discovery: identify where metrics live, what sources exist, what share of the organisation each covers
- Review any existing documents shared (org charts, team pages, existing engineering reports, dashboards)

### Day 2

- Continue data landscape discovery
- First pass at the measurement source map: for each of the 5 dimensions, what data exists and where?
- Note where proxies will be needed and what they'll be
- Begin tailoring interview questions by role (EM, tech lead, engineer questions are different)

### Days 3–4

- First wave of in-person or call interviews: engineering managers and tech leads
- Feed signals into the scoring framework as interviews complete — don't wait for all interviews before starting analysis
- End of Day 3 or start of Day 4: sponsor check-in (see below)

### Day 5

- Second wave of interviews: a sample of engineers (different questions — focused on experience, not management view)
- Chase async form responses
- Data source map is largely complete by end of Day 5

---

## Sponsor check-in — Day 3 or 4

This is not a formal readout. It is a short conversation (20–30 mins) with the person who commissioned the engagement.

**Purpose:**
1. Validate the signals coming up — does this match their sense of it, or is something surprising?
2. Understand how they plan to use the findings — this shapes how Week 2 is written

**On the appetite question:** ask "who is this report for and how will you use it?" not "how honest should I be?" One answer shapes the format. The other just makes it awkward. A sponsor who says "this is for the board" wants a different document than one who says "this is for me and my direct reports."

A sponsor who recognises what you're seeing and says "yes, that's exactly the problem" wants a frank report. One who hedges or redirects wants something more diplomatically framed. Both are valid — you need to know before you write.

---

## Week 2 — Analysis, scoring, report

### Days 6–7

- Complete any remaining data collection
- Close out async forms
- First pass at scoring each of the 5 dimensions
- Confidence level for each score based on data quality (high = real data, medium = proxy, low = interview signal only)

### Days 8–9

- Analysis and synthesis
- Draft findings for each dimension
- Write prioritised recommendations (what to address first and why)
- Draft the measurement source map as a deliverable

### Day 10

- Finalise report
- Prepare readout
- Readout with sponsor (and wider group if agreed)

---

## Outputs

1. Engineering effectiveness scorecard — organisation-wide and per team
2. Measurement source map — what was assessed, where metrics came from, confidence level per signal
3. Measurement maturity rating (0–5 scale)
4. Prioritised recommendations
5. 90-day plan skeleton

---

## Scaling notes

**Small org (1–3 teams):** All interviews can be in person or on calls. Data discovery is fast. Report may be shorter but findings are often sharper because signals are less diluted.

**Large org (10+ teams):** Data discovery alone can be a project. Scope deliberately — pick representative teams across different areas rather than attempting full coverage. The difficulty in finding coherent metrics across the org is itself a high-value finding. Do not attempt exhaustive coverage in 2 weeks.

**No existing metrics:** Proxy everything. Interview signal plus proxy data is enough to produce a credible scorecard. Be explicit about confidence levels. The absence of measurement infrastructure is itself the primary finding and recommendation.
